PAX — CHAPTERS 4–9 SUMMARY

Chapter 4

Peter secretly leaves his grandfather’s house to find Pax. His first night is cold and miserable because he forgot a flashlight and must sleep beside a culvert. He continues anyway, motivated by his concern for Pax.

After hours of walking, Peter reaches a small town. His dirty appearance and absence from school make people suspicious. He buys a flashlight, lies to a hardware-store employee, and hides at a school baseball field. The familiar setting comforts him, so he changes clothes, disguises himself with dirt, and crawls beneath a dugout bench to rest.

Peter is courageous and determined, but he is also poorly prepared. His love for Pax keeps him moving despite fear, exhaustion, and repeated mistakes.

Chapter 5

Pax meets his first wild foxes: Bristle, a cautious and territorial female, and Runt, her smaller, playful adopted brother. Bristle distrusts Pax because he smells like humans. Her successful hunt also shows that Pax lacks the survival skills of a wild fox.

Runt immediately befriends Pax, and they play with Peter’s plastic soldier. Bristle shows them an image of foxes injured in a steel trap, explaining her fear and hatred of humans. When a storm approaches, Runt invites Pax home. Bristle reluctantly permits him to stay for one night, calling him “Human-Stinker.” Pax still believes Peter will return and plans to go back to the road after the storm.

Pax is caught between two worlds: he belongs biologically among foxes, but his upbringing and loyalty bind him to Peter.

Chapter 6

Peter awakens when a baseball team arrives and blends into the crowd. He watches a talented, undersized shortstop. Baseball gives Peter a feeling of “holy calm” because the field has clear boundaries: anything inside the fence is his responsibility, while anything outside it is not.

This idea matters because Peter struggles with guilt and responsibility. His mother died in a car accident when he was seven after going shopping without him because he had been punished. Peter secretly wonders whether her death was his fault. He denied his anger when a therapist questioned him. Soon afterward, he found Pax, the only surviving kit beside his dead mother and siblings, and rescued him. Caring for Pax became tied to Peter’s grief, which helps explain why abandoning the fox feels unbearable.

Peter continues traveling, reaches the highway, and tries a westbound shortcut. After being frightened away from private property, he walks through wet, uneven ground in the dark. His foot catches on a root, a bone snaps, and the foot rapidly swells. He realizes he cannot walk.

Chapter 7

Pax returns to the road and waits for Peter. Although extremely hungry and thirsty, he refuses to abandon his post. An elderly fox named Gray approaches peacefully. Gray once lived among humans and tells Pax that soldiers have closed the road and that war is coming.

Gray leads Pax to water and describes war as a human sickness that makes people abandon their normal behavior and attack one another. He explains that war destroys animals and their habitats as well as human lives. Pax decides to travel south toward home before the war reaches Peter. Gray offers to accompany him while searching for a safer home for his own family.

Pax also considers human dishonesty. He recognizes that Peter’s father sometimes disguises anger and fear with friendly behavior, but insists that Peter is honest and considerate toward animals. Pax has now stopped merely waiting to be rescued and has begun taking action.

Chapter 8

Peter awakens in a barn and meets its owner, Vola, an intimidating woman who lives alone and has a wooden lower leg. She quickly recognizes Peter’s lies. A former military medic, Vola examines his swollen foot and identifies a clean fracture that must not bear weight for about six weeks.

Peter finally explains that his father volunteered for the war, his mother is dead, his father forced him to abandon Pax, and he is traveling roughly two hundred miles to rescue the fox. Vola says he cannot continue in his condition and should call his grandfather, but Peter refuses because Pax cannot wait six weeks.

Despite her harsh manner, Vola treats the injury and builds Peter wooden crutches. Peter initially steals her knife because he fears she might kill him, then feels ashamed when he realizes she is helping him. Offended by his suspicion, Vola orders him to leave and directs him toward a gas station.

Chapter 9

Gray and Pax return to the meadow and find an outsider challenging Gray for his territory, apparently to impress Bristle. Gray drives him away simply by announcing his presence. He then tells his pregnant mate that he will travel south with Pax to search for a safer home before the war arrives.

Runt is delighted to see Pax and brings Peter’s toy soldier, but Bristle remains hostile. When Pax says that his humans are not dangerous, Bristle insists that all humans are dangerous. She restrains Runt and prepares to tell the traumatic story behind her fear of people, a steel trap, and blood in the snow. The chapter ends as she begins.

Overall

Peter and Pax are both trying to repair their separation, but neither can complete the journey alone. Gray guides Pax and teaches him about war, while Vola treats Peter and gives him crutches. Pax must reconsider his trust in humans, and Peter must reconsider his distrust of Vola. Both are learning about survival, responsibility, belonging, and accepting help.
