\section*{Answer Key}

\subsection*{Part A: \textit{Pax}, Chapter 4}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{B} --- Not hunting for his grandfather's flashlight. Without it, the moon lit his way only about two hours before it drowned beneath thick clouds; he stumbled in the dark another hour before giving up, losing most of a night's travel.
  \item He slit one open into a long mat to lie on and cut the other into a poncho against the cold mist, using his mitt as a pillow. He is resourceful, but he is improvising because he packed badly.
  \item \textbf{C} --- Maybe seven or eight miles so far; about twenty more miles before the highway.
  \item His grandfather won't be home from work for a whole day, so no one will even suspect he is gone yet.
  \item \textbf{A} --- His reflection --- hair tangled with leaves, sweatshirt streaked in mud, nose reddening toward a full-faced sunburn. ``The kid in the window looked like a runaway---one who hadn't prepared very well.'' His hopeful mood melts.
  \item The man peers down his nose the way a hawk Peter once saw searched for prey from the top of a cedar. Peter feels hunted --- like small prey being watched by a predator.
  \item \textbf{D} --- He is covering for why he isn't in school. It only half works: the man nods, but then follows him inside, hovers while he shops, shadows him at checkout, and follows him outside to point him toward the school.
  \item He is becoming more superstitious and more willing to trust feelings over logic; fear and exhaustion are wearing down his usual practical thinking.
  \item It shows Pax is constantly on Peter's mind --- he understands the world by imagining what his fox would do. It also quietly justifies the risky decision to stop and rest.
  \item He is far more exhausted than he admits. The promise sounds like reassurance but signals that he \textit{will} fall asleep and lose time --- exactly what happens in Chapter 6, where he loses over three hours.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Part B: \textit{Pax}, Chapter 5}
\begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{10}
  \item \textbf{A} --- ``This is my territory,'' followed by ``I hunt here.'' Ears and tail held erect mean she expects his submission.
  \item He was orphaned and rescued by Peter at only sixteen days old, his eyes barely opened. He was born with the same instinct to distrust, but ``distrust is no match for kindness administered consistently and unmeasured,'' so he approaches instead of fleeing.
  \item Whether she has seen his boy --- he shares images of Peter's round ears, impossibly long legs, and black curled hair. Finding Peter matters more to him than territory, food, or safety.
  \item \textbf{B} --- Her ears prick at a rustling in broom sedge, her rear twitches gathering energy, she springs high with paws tight over her black nose, dives into the grass, comes up with the rat in her jaws, and bites through its neck. She is a competent wild hunter; Pax, raised in a pen, has never even eaten raw prey.
  \item \textbf{D} --- His brimming kibble bowl, Peter's hand-fed treats, and the ultimate reward: peanut butter. He concludes he needs to find his boy, because ``his boy would feed him.''
  \item The memory of his humans' car roaring away in its stinging spray of gravel. It is the moment he was abandoned, so any creature leaving him now reopens that wound.
  \item Runt is Bristle's smaller, skinnier brother --- ``brother but not littermate.'' He announces \textit{Friendly\dots{} Play!} and welcomes Pax ``as though they had played together since birth,'' while Bristle bares her teeth and warns \textit{Dangerous. Stay away.}
  \item \textbf{C} --- He has climbed the walls of his pen, but never higher than six feet. It shows how limited his life has been --- he didn't even know foxes could ``soar like birds.''
  \item A cold, howling wind; a mated pair of foxes struggling with something like steel but with jaws and clamps instead of bars; the steel jaws and snowy ground smeared with blood. They were caught in human traps --- the source of Bristle's hatred and fear of humans.
  \item ``One night only, Human-Stinker.'' He may shelter with them only for the storm. Pax privately promises that he will follow his scent back to the road after the storm, that his humans will come for him, and that once he finds his boy he will never leave his side.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Part C: \textit{Pax}, Chapter 6}
\begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{20}
  \item \textbf{B} --- The footfalls of just-released kids, their hoots, and fists thumping into gloves. He is too late because twenty boys and their coach are already streaming down the hill and uniformed adults are up at the parking lot, so he cannot slip away unseen and must blend in on the bleachers.
  \item A small kid with a straw-colored crew cut and a bleached-out red T-shirt, playing shortstop. While everyone else scrambles ``like puppies,'' he stands like a statue, hands poised waist high, eyes glued to the bat, and springs the instant ``wood smacked cowhide.'' He reaches every ball near his territory despite being short enough to look like someone's tag-along kid brother.
  \item \textbf{C} --- ``Holy'' came the closest and ``calm'' was in the mix, but neither was exactly right. It feels too private, and he doesn't think he has the words to explain it.
  \item The fence behind his territory. It tells him exactly what is his responsibility and what isn't --- inside the fence he'd better field it; over the fence isn't his to worry about. ``Nice and clear.'' He wishes responsibility had such bright tall fences around it off the field, too.
  \item He pulled little cars and trucks from a toy box (there must have been a hundred) and crashed them together, two by two. Every time she said: ``That must have been hard for you. Your mom gets in a car to go buy groceries, a regular day, and she never comes home.''
  \item \textbf{B} --- ``No\dots{} Never.'' The narrator says flatly: ``A lie.''
  \item That he had done something terrible, and that as punishment his mother hadn't taken him to the store --- and that she blamed him for what happened. He secretly believes her death was his fault, which explains his buried anger, his guilt, and his desperate need to protect Pax.
  \item He found a fox run over by the side of the road near his house. So soon after his mother's coffin was lowered into the ground, he felt an unshakable need to bury the body. Looking for a good place, he found the den with three cold, stiff kit bodies and one little ball of gray fur still warm and breathing. He tucked Pax into his sweatshirt pocket and told his dad --- \textit{said}, not asked --- ``I'm keeping him.''
  \item He lies to cover his runaway tracks and hide where he is really going. The boy assumes he is scouting before Saturday's game, so he spits and calls him a jerk. Peter feels lousy because he had just done something kind --- staying to help a kid the team abandoned --- and his lie destroyed a genuine connection with someone who understood the same ``holy calm'' he feels.
  \item He wastes over three hours sleeping; leaving the field at 4:15, he hurries through town, scales a fence, reaches the highway at about 4:50, crosses it, and takes a dirt wagon road that turns out to lead to someone's barn instead of a shortcut. He rests, eats, watches fourteen deer, and is startled off when a saw screeches. Fleeing the headlights on the highway, he ducks into spongy peat ground, sinks his foot into swamp water (soaking his boots --- ``another mistake''), and while clambering back to higher ground his right foot catches on a root. He hears the bone break, a soft muffled snap. It is catastrophic because his whole plan depends on walking roughly twenty more miles to Pax --- and Pax, who has never eaten raw prey and is waiting by the road for humans who aren't coming, has no one else.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Part F: Subject-Verb Agreement}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item is --- subject is \textit{box}
  \item was --- \textit{everyone} is singular
  \item drives --- \textit{nor} makes the verb agree with \textit{mother}
  \item are --- subject is \textit{reasons}
  \item requires --- \textit{mathematics} is singular
  \item have --- \textit{both} is plural
  \item meets --- collective noun acting as one unit
  \item come --- subject is \textit{floats}
  \item needs --- \textit{each} is singular
  \item were --- verb agrees with \textit{players}
  \item seems --- an amount treated as one sum
  \item is --- \textit{news} is singular
  \item are --- subject is \textit{keys}
  \item was --- \textit{some} with the singular mass noun \textit{cake}
  \item were --- \textit{some} with the plural \textit{cookies}
  \item is --- one person holding two roles
  \item sits --- subject is \textit{stack}
  \item meet --- \textit{few} is plural
  \item remains --- a title is singular
  \item disagree --- members acting individually (``among themselves'')
  \item handle --- verb agrees with \textit{assistants}
  \item has --- \textit{none} with the singular mass noun \textit{water}
