\section*{Part C: Subject--Verb Agreement}
\textit{Circle the correct verb.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The carton of eggs (sit / sits) on the top shelf.
  \item Each of the bicycles (was / were) repainted last spring.
  \item Omar and Lucy (walks / walk) to school together.
  \item Neither the players nor the coach (know / knows) the score.
  \item There (is / are) four exits in this building.
  \item The news from the hospital (worry / worries) my mother.
  \item A row of tall hedges (block / blocks) the view.
  \item Both of the puppies (need / needs) a bath.
  \item Either Dana or her cousins (host / hosts) the party.
  \item The scratches on the table (show / shows) its age.

\subsection*{Part B: Subject and Predicate}
\textit{CS = complete subject, CP = complete predicate, SS = simple subject, SP = simple predicate.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item CS: The exhausted swimmer \quad CP: floated on her back \quad SS: swimmer \quad SP: floated
  \item CS: Jonah and Priya \quad CP: shared a locker all year \quad SS: Jonah, Priya (compound) \quad SP: shared
  \item CS: an empty trash can \quad CP: Down the driveway rolled \quad SS: can \quad SP: rolled \quad (inverted order)
  \item CS: The steady hum of the refrigerator \quad CP: annoyed everyone \quad SS: hum \quad SP: annoyed
  \item CS: My brother's sprained wrist \quad CP: ached for weeks \quad SS: wrist \quad SP: ached
  \item CS: The careful electrician \quad CP: tested every outlet \quad SS: electrician \quad SP: tested
  \item CS: a broken lawnmower \quad CP: Behind the garage sat \quad SS: lawnmower \quad SP: sat \quad (inverted order)
  \item CS: The referee \quad CP: paused the game and checked the clock \quad SS: referee \quad SP: paused, checked (compound)
  \item CS: The radio and the newspaper \quad CP: reported the same story \quad SS: radio, newspaper (compound) \quad SP: reported
  \item CS: A tall, quiet exchange student \quad CP: joined our class \quad SS: student \quad SP: joined
  \item CS: a stray kitten \quad CP: Under the porch hid \quad SS: kitten \quad SP: hid \quad (inverted order)
  \item CS: The retired pilot \quad CP: trusted her own instincts \quad SS: pilot \quad SP: trusted
  \item CS: Ines \quad CP: packed her lunch and caught the early bus \quad SS: Ines \quad SP: packed, caught (compound)
  \item CS: The smell of fresh bread \quad CP: filled the hallway \quad SS: smell \quad SP: filled
  \item CS: My grandfather's four acres of orchard \quad CP: needed constant care \quad SS: acres \quad SP: needed
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Notes:} 1, 6, 7, 10, and 14 turn on the true subject (\emph{carton}, \emph{news}, \emph{row},
\emph{scratches}, \emph{most} = most of the milk), not the noun nearest the verb. In 4 and 9,
``neither/nor'' and ``either/or'' agree with the \emph{nearer} subject. In 5 and 13 the subject
follows the verb (\emph{exits}, \emph{runners}). In 12, \emph{nine dollars} is a single amount, so
it takes a singular verb.

\textbf{Rewrites:}
\begin{enumerate}[start=16]
  \item The stack of towels \textbf{was} still damp.
  \item Neither Ravi nor Celia \textbf{remembers} the address.
  \item There \textbf{are} two elevators in the lobby.
  \item Each of the campers \textbf{brings} a flashlight.
  \item The instructions printed on the box \textbf{were} confusing.
\end{enumerate}
