\section*{Part C: Subject--Verb Agreement}
\noindent\textit{Circle the correct verb.}
\begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{35}

  \item Each of the windows (need / needs) new caulking.
  \item Rosa and Tom\'as (rides / ride) the same bus.
  \item Neither the students nor the teacher (want / wants) a quiz.
  \item There (was / were) three messages on the machine.
  \item The mathematics of the design (confuse / confuses) me.
  \item A bundle of newspapers (sit / sits) by the curb.
  \item Both of the recipes (call / calls) for buttermilk.
  \item Either the manager or the clerks (open / opens) the store.
  \item The stains on the ceiling (mark / marks) an old leak.

\noindent\textit{Notes:} 36, 42, 45, and 49 turn on the true subject (\textit{bouquet}, \textit{bundle}, \textit{stains}, \textit{half} = half of the cake), not the noun nearest the verb. In 39 and 44, ``neither/nor'' and ``either/or'' agree with the \textit{nearer} subject (\textit{teacher}, \textit{clerks}). In 40 and 48 the subject follows the verb (\textit{messages}, \textit{ferries}). In 41, \textit{mathematics} looks plural but names one subject. In 47, \textit{twenty minutes} is a single span of time, so it takes a singular verb.
